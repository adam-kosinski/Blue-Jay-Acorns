# Blue Jay Acorns — Specs and Plans

## Vision

A game about a blue jay burying acorns.

## Current milestone

Entities (acorn, oak tree), five trees at start, random ground grass, a world 10× the view width with a following camera, and a restart button that regenerates the starting state.

**Status:** implemented. Dev server: `npm run dev`. Scene lives in `src/game/`.

## Stack

- React (Vite dev server)
- 2D scene rendered on an HTML canvas, driven by `requestAnimationFrame` (no game engine yet)

## Scene and world

- Viewport is the browser window. The **world** is larger and the camera scrolls.
- World **width** is 10× the window width at generation (start or restart).
- World **height**: from the ground up, enough that a camera locked to the top of the world no longer shows the ground, plus extra sky (`WORLD_TOP_EXTRA_FACTOR` of the view height).
- **Ground** stays a strip along the bottom of the world. Standing still looks like the original prototype (ground along the bottom of the view).
- **Camera** tries to keep the player centered horizontally and vertically. If that would show area outside the world, it clamps to the world bounds (player can sit off-center at edges). Flying up therefore transitions from “ground-pinned” to “player-centered.”
- Player is a filled square, **1/8 of the window height**.
- On-screen debug label shows the current locomotion state.
- **Restart** regenerates trees, acorns, grass, world size (from the current window), and player pose.

## Entities

- **Acorn** — small brown circle; diameter is **1/4 of player height**. Scattered on the ground at start so the entity is visible.
- **Oak tree** — about **one window tall**: tall brown rectangle trunk with a green circle of leaves on top. **Five** spawn at start, spread across the world.
- **Grass** — randomly generated blades/tufts along the ground for a nature pattern.

## Player locomotion

States:

- **standing** — the sprite is in contact with the ground.
- **flying** — the sprite is above the ground and hovering (no gravity while airborne).

Transitions:

- Touching the ground (bottom of sprite at ground line) → **standing**.
- From **standing**, press **W** → take off: rise to a hover altitude above the ground and enter **flying**. The bird stays at that height until the player climbs or descends.

Controls (held keys, polled each frame):

| Key | Standing                   | Flying                              |
| --- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| W   | Take off to hover altitude | Climb (increase altitude)           |
| S   | No effect                  | Descend; cannot go below the ground |
| A   | Move left                  | Move left                           |
| D   | Move right                 | Move right                          |

Horizontal movement is allowed in both states. The player is clamped to the **world** edges, not the viewport.

## Assumptions (open to change)

- W while already flying climbs freely; it is not limited to the initial hover height.
- Flying has no gravity: releasing keys holds altitude.
- A tap of W while standing auto-rises to a fixed hover offset, even if W is released mid-ascent. Holding W continues climbing past that height. S during takeoff cancels the auto-rise and descends.
- After a partial descent, releasing S holds the new altitude (does not snap back to the original hover height).
- No collision with trees or acorns yet (overlap is allowed).
- Placeholder art (geometry only); no burying gameplay yet.
- World size is baked at start/restart from the current window; resizing the window does not rebuild the world until Restart.

## Next (not this milestone)

- Blue jay sprite / animation
- Burying acorns, collecting, more gameplay

## Update: blue jay and acorn mechanics

- Takeoff hover is 1/3 of the old height; horizontal speed is 2× vertical (`MOVE_SPEED_IN_SIZES = 2 × VERTICAL_SPEED_IN_SIZES`, vertical lowered to 2.7).
- Acorns are drawn last (in front of everything). They spawn in Gaussian clusters around the oak trunks (round-robin per tree).
- Mini-map (top center): trees, ground acorns, camera rectangle, bird dot.
- Cartoon blue jay drawn on canvas in `render.ts` (`drawBird`): walk cycle (leg swing, hop, folded wing) and fly cycle (flapping wing, tucked legs); faces movement direction.
- **S on the ground** (fresh key-down only; held-S carry-over from flight is ignored; while flying S still descends): pick up the nearest ground acorn within reach, or drop the held one (it falls). One acorn at a time. **O is removed.**
- **B** (standing, fresh press): holding → bury ahead of the bird (mound shown); empty-beaked → dig up a buried acorn near the bird into the beak.
- **Held acorn** is drawn inside `drawBird`, behind the body, with the beak open (separate upper/lower mandibles). Ground acorns still draw in front of everything.
- Logic in `src/game/acorns.ts`; key edges (`{ s, space, e }`) in `input.ts`.

## Update: hunger

- Hunger bar (top right, left of Restart): drains from full over `HUNGER_DRAIN_SECONDS` (120s). Green above the threshold marker (halfway), yellow below it.
- **E**: only when below the threshold and holding an acorn: ~0.7s eating animation (beak chomps, head bobs, acorn shrinks), then the acorn is consumed and the bar refills by half (`ACORN_NUTRITION`). S/B are ignored while eating. Reaching 0 has no penalty yet.
- Logic in `src/game/hunger.ts`; constants in `constants.ts`.

## Update: terrain, scenery, forest backdrop

- **Terrain** (`terrain.ts`): fractal 1D Perlin noise (2 octaves, amplitude 0.04 of view height) precomputed every 8px into a `Float32Array` on `world.terrain`; `groundYAt(terrain, x)` interpolates. `world.groundY` is only the flat reference line. Everything (player standing/landing/hover, acorns, trees, props, mounds, minimap) uses the local height. Standing sticks to the ground on slopes; only W lifts off.
- **Ground** is a brown dirt-gradient polygon; grass is replaced by **leaf litter** (small rotated ellipses in browns/oranges, on and just below the surface, culled to the view).
- **Props** (generated per run in `world.ts`, drawn after trees and before the bird, no collision): 28 bushes (green lobe clusters), 9 logs (tilted to the slope, end-grain rings), 12 boulders and ~22 clusters of 1-3 small rocks (jittered half-ellipse polygons with a highlight), sized relative to player size.
- **Background**: hazy green gradient plus 5 parallax forest layers (factors 0.12, 0.22, 0.35, 0.5, 0.68; fog 0.8 down to 0.12 blending toward the haze color; farther layers lifted higher). Each layer fills solid from its base to the screen bottom, with `base = groundY - maxCamY*(1-p) - camY*p - lift*viewHeight`, so with the camera pinned to the ground it meets the ground line. Layer x on screen = layerX - camera.x*p. Foreground oaks stay the darkest.
- Minimap: green background, brown terrain polygon.

## Update: NPC blue jays

- 5 NPC jays (`npc.ts`, `world.npcs`) with random `nosiness` (0-1, no effect yet) and their own hunger bar; both are annotated above each NPC along with its state. NPCs are drawn before the player and shown as orange dots on the minimap.
- They run a finite state machine (explore, gather, eat, find burial spot, bury; follow/hide are placeholders) driven by simulated W/A/S/D keys through the shared `updatePlayer`. Probabilistic decisions run once per second. Full chart: [NPC_STATE_MACHINE.md](NPC_STATE_MACHINE.md) (keep it in sync).
- Background parallax trees now match the foreground oaks: about one viewport tall, trunk plus one round crown (scale 0.9-1.0 by layer).

## Update: trees, terrain, idle, B key

- **B** (not Space) buries / digs up; `KeyEdges` is `{ s, b, e }`.
- **Oaks** are generated per tree (`oak.ts`): leaning tapered trunk, random asymmetric branches with sub-branches, and many leaf-lobe clusters in four tones. There are now 10 oaks (acorns doubled to 32). The parallax layers use the same generator, blended toward haze, plus bush and log silhouettes along each layer's base.
- **Terrain** uses two Perlin scales: a large one (wavelength 6 view heights, amplitude 0.5) for land shape and a small one (0.3 view heights, amplitude 0.09) for local bumps. Terrain only rises above the flat `groundY`.
- Held acorn: beak opens wider (gap 8.5 sprite units).
- NPCs gained an **idle** state (see NPC_STATE_MACHINE.md); there are now 3 NPCs (see the latest notes below).
- Parallax layers each have their own rolling horizon (terrain flattened and phase-shifted by distance); layer trees, bushes and logs stand on it, so background trunks meet ground instead of floating.

## Update: performance and layer heights

- Each parallax layer is pre-rendered once into a wide offscreen bitmap (`forestStripFor` in `render.ts`: horizon, trees, bushes, logs) and blitted per frame, cropped to the visible rows. Foreground oaks are cached as sprites (`drawOak` in `oak.ts`, LRU of 80). Leaf litter is sorted by x and found with a binary search.
- Background layers sit higher (lifts 0.4/0.33/0.26/0.2/0.14 of view height) and terrain amplitudes are lower (0.2 large, 0.04 small) so the background ground is usually visible above the foreground ground.
- Background layers use brown trunks/logs and brown ground (the foreground colors blended toward haze by distance), with green bushes and leaves.
- Oaks (and so the parallax trees) are 1.5-2x taller than the earlier design, about 18% keep one or two low branches near the ground. Tree bitmaps are capped at 4 megapixels. Bushes are mostly rejected within 3 player sizes of an oak (12% keep chance).
- Walking speed is half of flying speed (`WALK_SPEED_FACTOR`); applies to NPCs too.
- Farther parallax layers pack trees denser (per-layer `spacing`, an average trunk gap of 0.11 (farthest, densest) to 0.36 (nearest) view heights, in `FOREST_LAYERS`).

## Update: hopping, acorn actions, NPC pacing, tree acorn drops

- **Hopping**: a standing bird moves in discrete hops (`hopOnGround` in `player.ts`). Distance accumulates at the old walking speed while A/D are held and is spent in hops of `HOP_LENGTH_IN_SIZES` (0.9 sizes, `HOP_DURATION` 0.2 s); the first press hops immediately and a hop in progress always finishes. The sprite hops up with legs tucked (cache key `h` + 10 phases). NPCs use the same movement; their ground arrival radius is 0.5 sizes.
- **Acorn animations**: pick-up (`PICKUP_DURATION`) is one dip of the head toward the acorn; bury and dig-up (`BURY_DURATION`) are three pecks with dirt flicking up. The acorn changes hands at the halfway point (`PlayerAction`, `startPickup`/`startBury`/`startDig`/`updateAction` in `acorns.ts`); the bird stays put meanwhile and the action cancels if another bird took the acorn first. Pick-up and dig reach are horizontal only (`REACH_IN_SIZES` 0.9). Head tilt keeps its world angle except for an extra downward turn during a dip.
- **NPC pacing**: NPCs pause 1 s (`NPC_WAIT_SECONDS`) after picking up an acorn, after landing to bury, and when entering explore. Idle NPCs sometimes hop or fly a short way (1.5-5 sizes).
- **Idle logic**: every time an NPC would enter explore or idle it checks hunger: above 0.9 (`NPC_IDLE_HUNGER`) it idles; an idle NPC whose hunger drops below 0.9 explores. NPCs also use this check at spawn.
- **Tree acorn drops**: each second every oak has a `ACORN_DROP_CHANCE_PER_SECOND` (0.0003) chance to drop 3-10 acorns from random points in its canopy lobes; they fall with gravity (`updateTreeDrops`). World cap `ACORN_WORLD_CAP` 160. That is about 1 acorn per minute worldwide, near what the 5 NPCs eat once the starting acorns are gone (headless runs: 5-8 starting acorns eaten in the first two minutes, then none).
- Flow chart updated with decision diamonds: see NPC_STATE_MACHINE.md.

- Follow-up tuning: hops are continuous (`HOP_DURATION` 0.3 s, length derived as `MOVE_SPEED_IN_SIZES * WALK_SPEED_FACTOR * HOP_DURATION` so the ground speed is exactly half the flying speed, 2.7 sizes/s); while A/D stay held the next hop starts the instant one ends, and the feet stay visible (they trail back and lift in the air). Pick-up is faster (`PICKUP_DURATION` 0.3 s). Pecking stretches the neck forward and down so the beak tip touches the ground at `BEAK_REACH_X` (54 sprite units ahead of the bird's center): the bird slides sideways during the first 40% of a pick-up or dig-up so the beak lands on the acorn, and the burial spot is where the beak touches. Animation poses are quantized finer (30 action phases, 16 hop phases) so they look smoother; the bird sprite cache holds up to 300 poses.
- NPC jays fly, climb and hop at `NPC_SPEED_SCALE` (0.75) of the main bird's speed (`updatePlayer(..., speedScale)`; hop length scales, hop time does not). Hops are about 0.75 player sizes (`HOP_DURATION` 0.28 s, 0.76 size for the main bird, 0.57 for NPCs) with the same average ground speed rule. During a hop the body keeps its posture and only the legs rotate slightly (0.2 rad) about the hips: back at launch, straight at the top, forward at landing; the feet stay visible.
- Within each hop the horizontal speed eases from 1.4x the average at launch down to 0.6x at landing (average unchanged).
- World is half as wide (`WORLD_WIDTH_MULTIPLIER` 5) with 4 NPC jays. To keep the same density the oak count (5), starting acorns (16), bushes (14), logs (5), boulders (6) and rock clusters (11) were halved too, and the acorn drop chance was raised to 0.0005 so the fewer oaks still supply about 1 acorn a minute.
- Hunger: the full bar drains in 60 s (`HUNGER_DRAIN_SECONDS`), eating is allowed below 2/3 full (`HUNGER_THRESHOLD`, also the HUD marker and the NPC eat check), and one acorn refills 1/3 of the bar (`ACORN_NUTRITION`). The NPC idle threshold stays at 0.9. Earlier notes in this file that mention 120 s, halfway and half a bar are superseded.
- The hunger bar is large (up to 520 px wide, 32 px tall) at the bottom center above the hint text, with the 2/3 marker line and a bold label ("Hunger", "Hungry - find an acorn", "Hungry - press E to eat"). At 1/5 full or less (`STARVING_LEVEL`) it flashes red (every 0.3 s) with a pulsing red outline and the label reads "Starving!".
- Lag spikes: the expensive cached bitmaps are now built ahead of time instead of when something first scrolls into view or first appears (`warmUpScene` in `render.ts`, stepped once per frame from `GameCanvas.tsx`, also after Restart): the five parallax strips, all the oaks (`getOakSprite` in `oak.ts`), and the common jay poses (24 flying phases, 16 hop phases and standing, with and without a held acorn; `birdSpriteFor`). In a scroll-through test in the software-rendered pane the worst frame went from about 790 ms (five frames over 100 ms) to 72 ms (none over 100 ms). Rare poses (eating, pecking) are still built on first use.
- The mini-map also shows rocks (gray domes), logs (dark brown bars) and bushes (green domes) as tiny low-detail shapes on the ground line.
- Game over: when the player's hunger bar reaches 0 the simulation freezes and `drawGameOver` (in `render.ts`) fades in a vignette and a parchment card with a wooden double border that eases in, the title "You got a rumbly in your tumbly" (sized to fit), a divider with three acorns, the line "The jay went hungry. Click Restart to try again.", and a pulsing "Restart ↗" pointer toward the button. The `gameOver` flag lives in `GameCanvas.tsx`; Restart clears it. NPC hunger reaching 0 has no penalty.
- NPC memory: each NPC records the x of every acorn it buries (`Npc.memory`). An exploring NPC whose hunger drops below `NPC_RETRIEVE_HUNGER` (0.2) and that remembers at least one spot enters the new `retrieve` state (`runRetrieve` in `npc.ts`): it flies to the nearest remembered spot, lands, hops to it, forgets the spot, and digs if an acorn is still buried within 0.25 sizes of it (another bird may have taken it). If not, it tries the next nearest spot until memory is empty, then goes back to explore/idle; if it digs one up it pauses 1 s and eats it. Flow chart updated.
- Buried-acorn mounds keep the original small half-ellipse shape, drawn in a dark brown (`#472b14`), darker than the dirt, so they stand out. They are drawn before the bushes, so a bush overlapping one still hides it.
- Bug fix: NPCs could end up exploring with an acorn in the beak and 0 hunger. The eat state compared against a stale `eatAttempted` flag left over from the previous meal, so after the first meal an NPC sent to eat skipped eating and was returned to explore at once, acorn in beak. The eat state now starts and finishes on its own (`startEating` in `updateNpcs`). Also added overrides: a held acorn plus hunger below the threshold always forces the eat state, and a held acorn that cannot be eaten sends an exploring or idle NPC to bury it. After this fix NPCs actually eat after every pickup, so they eat far more than before (headless: 240 eat-state samples over 8 25-minute runs); you may want to raise `ACORN_DROP_CHANCE_PER_SECOND`.

## Update: window-independent distances, drop rate, perception, debug panel

- **No window-tied distances.** The game now runs in a fixed logical view `LOGICAL_VIEW_HEIGHT` (800) tall, scaled to the window (`scale` in `GameCanvas.tsx`; the canvas transform is `dpr * scale`). The logical width is `innerWidth / scale`, so a wider window just shows more world. Player size, world size and every gameplay distance are therefore independent of the window. The world width is `viewHeight * TYPICAL_ASPECT_RATIO (16:9) * WORLD_WIDTH_MULTIPLIER`, never the window width. Resizing only changes the scale (and re-runs the bitmap warm-up); nothing is regenerated or resynced.
- **NPC perception** (`nearestFreeAcorn` in `npc.ts`): an NPC perceives free acorns up to `NPC_PERCEIVE_AHEAD_IN_SIZES` (a third of a typical screen: `TYPICAL_VIEW_WIDTH_IN_SIZES / 3`, about 4.74 sizes) in front of it (by facing) and `NPC_PERCEIVE_BEHIND_IN_SIZES` (1 size) behind it, with the same vertical reach. The burial trip is up to `NPC_BURIAL_SPAN_IN_SIZES` (two typical screen widths). `viewWidth` is no longer passed to the NPC code.
- **Acorn drop rate**: `ACORN_DROP_CHANCE_PER_SECOND` is hard-coded to 0.00256 per oak per second: the expected 5/60 acorns per second (the five birds' hunger decrease rates, 1/60 each) divided by 6.5 acorns per group and 5 oaks.
- **Debug panel** (top-left, HTML radio buttons in `GameCanvas.tsx`): "Deficit (-25%)", "Default", "Surplus (+25%)" scale the drop chance by 0.75, 1 and 1.25 (`ACORN_DROP_MODES`). The choice is saved in `localStorage` (`blue-jay-acorns:drop-mode`), so it survives Restart and page reloads. The old state/beak text in the top-left corner is gone.
- Controls changed: **Space** picks up / drops an acorn (standing, fresh press) and **left Shift** (not right Shift) buries / digs up; S is only descend again (it no longer picks up) and B does nothing. E still eats. `KeyEdges` is now `{ pickup, bury, e }` (`input.ts` reads `event.code` for Space and ShiftLeft); the on-screen hint reads "WASD move · Space pick up / drop · Shift bury / dig up · E eat when hungry". Earlier notes in this file that mention S or B for these actions are superseded.
- **NPC noticing and priorities**: each free acorn that newly enters an NPC's view gets a single roll (`NPC_NOTICE_PROBABILITY` 0.75, `updatePerception` in `npc.ts`) for whether the bird notices it; noticed acorns stay noticed while in view and are forgotten on leaving view or being taken. An exploring NPC always goes for the nearest noticed acorn first (even when starving); only when none is in view and hunger is below 0.2 does it retrieve from its nearest remembered burial spot. The old per-second gather roll (`NPC_GATHER_PROBABILITY`) is gone. When an NPC starts exploring it pauses 1 s: the first half facing away from its travel direction and the second half facing it, so both sides get checked (perception depends on facing); it does not move during the pause.
- Dig tolerance for a remembered spot: the NPC must be within `ARRIVE_IN_SIZES` (0.5 player sizes) horizontally of the remembered x to arrive, and a buried acorn counts as still there only if its x is within 0.25 player sizes of the remembered x. The beak then slides up to 0.8 sizes forward or 0.5 back to reach it.
- Controls changed again: the **Down arrow** picks up an acorn (standing, fresh press, only when the beak is empty) and **Q** drops the held acorn; **Space** no longer does anything. Left Shift still buries / digs up, E eats, S is only descend. `KeyEdges` is now `{ pickup, drop, bury, e }`; the hint reads "WASD move · ↓ pick up · Q drop · Shift bury / dig up · E eat when hungry". This supersedes the previous Space/Shift note.
- Dig rule: a dig action is not tied to a burial spot or acorn. ## NPC blue jay behaviour reference (moved out of NPC_STATE_MACHINE.md, which now holds only the chart and a few notes)

The details below accompany the chart in `NPC_STATE_MACHINE.md`. They were its introduction and trailing sections.

- Most probabilistic transitions are rolled **once per second** per NPC; the follow rolls happen when an NPC becomes eligible or a bird enters its view, and the patience roll every `PATIENCE_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (see `follow`); everything else is checked every frame.
- **Noticing:** a standing bird always notices every free acorn in view (so on landing everything in view counts as seen). A flying bird gets one roll (`NPC_NOTICE_PROBABILITY` = 0.75) for each acorn that newly enters its view. Once noticed, an acorn is **remembered even after it leaves view** and never rolls again; the bird remembers the spot where it last saw it. When that spot comes back into view and the acorn is no longer there, it is forgotten immediately (the bird does not have to land). Until then a taken acorn is still remembered and still draws the bird's explore direction. An acorn that failed its roll can roll again when it re-enters view. What is in view depends on the direction the bird faces.
- Acorn perception: a third of a typical (16:9) screen width ahead and about one bird-width behind (`NPC_PERCEIVE_AHEAD_IN_SIZES` / `NPC_PERCEIVE_BEHIND_IN_SIZES`, measured in player sizes so it never depends on the window). Hunger thresholds: eat below 2/3 (`HUNGER_THRESHOLD`), idle above 0.9 (`NPC_IDLE_HUNGER`).
- Pauses (`NPC_WAIT_SECONDS` = 1 s, no movement or action): after picking up an acorn, after landing to bury, and when entering explore.
- Walking on the ground is done in hops; picking up, burying and digging play short animations (`PICKUP_DURATION`, `BURY_DURATION`).
- Right after noticing something new (a free acorn or burial site) an NPC decides at once instead of waiting for the next once-per-second decision; and while exploring with nothing in view it turns toward the nearest acorn or burial site it remembers.
- **Override:** a bird holding an acorn while below the eat threshold always switches to **eat**, whatever state it was in, except while finding a burial spot or burying, where it only eats the acorn when starving (below `NPC_STARVING_HUNGER`, 1/3, the same threshold that sends it to retrieve). A bird holding an acorn it cannot eat (hunger above the threshold) in explore or idle goes to find a burial spot instead.
- Each NPC remembers the x position of every acorn it buries (`Player.burials`, kept for every bird); a bird knows exactly where each of its acorns went, so a burial leaves its list only when that very acorn is gone: the bird digs up its own acorn (even while digging for another bird's site), a dig of its own comes up empty (no acorn within dig range, so any burial remembered there was taken), or it sees another bird dig up one of its own acorns (the dig in its view: about 4.7 sizes ahead, 1 behind). That dig is not noted as another bird's site either: nothing of anyone else's is left there. Only that one burial is forgotten. **Burying and digging both act exactly at the beak tip** (the bird's centre plus 54 sprite units in its facing direction, scaled by size; `beakTipX`). A dig targets the buried acorn closest to the beak tip within `DIG_RESPONSE_IN_SIZES` (1/4 of a player size, hardcoded; it used to be 1/3 around wherever the bird was told to dig, briefly 0.84); if that acorn is not exactly under the beak the bird shuffles sideways during the first part of the animation so that its beak lands on it. With nothing in range the bird digs where it stands and nothing comes up. NPCs put their beak exactly on the spot they mean to bury or dig at before acting (`moveBeakTo`: fly and land with the centre offset by the beak reach, hop the last stretch, turn with a one-frame tap if needed). The player always gets the nearest one. An NPC digging at another bird's site (gather) never brings up one of its own acorns (`PlayerAction.avoidOwn`): the nearest other acorn in range comes up, or nothing (an empty dig then makes it forget other-bird sites in range, but keeps its own burial memory when an own acorn is there); only in the retrieve state may it dig up its own.
- **Noticing a burial site** (`perceiveBuried`): when another bird's buried acorn (never the bird's own, which it already knows) newly comes into view, the bird may notice it with a probability that falls with the hops that disguised the mound (`burialSiteVisibility` in `vision.ts`, an exponential decay pinned to 0 at the 10th hop: 1, .60, .36, .22, .13, .08, .04, .02, .01, .004, 0 for 0..10 hops; `BURIAL_NOTICE_DECAY_PER_HOP` = 0.5; noticing burying and digging in progress is unaffected) times, while flying, the 0.75 notice scalar; a bird that lands with the site still in view rolls once more without that penalty. A noticed site joins the other-bird burial site list below (never for the exact spot of one of the bird's own remembered burials: the two lists never share a spot, and own burials are only visited when retrieving; a spot merely near an own burial can still be another bird's site) (a site it already remembers is not rolled for).
- **Other birds' burial sites:** when any bird (the player or another NPC) is burying an acorn or digging one up, an NPC can notice it at **any moment from the action's halfway point to its end** (when the head touches the ground while burying, or the acorn enters the beak while digging; an attempted dig that finds nothing is never noticed) (the first moment the spot is in view, e.g. after turning or landing; one roll per observer per action, same view as for free acorns: standing = always, flying = 75%, but a flying bird that missed its roll notices anyway once it lands with the spot in view) and remembers the spot in `sites` and a dashed white line from the NPC to the spot is drawn and fades over `NOTICE_LINE_FADE_SECONDS` (2 s), following the bird as it moves. The site is remembered without needing to be noticed again. While in view it is treated like a free acorn: the NPC gathers it by digging there (a dig is tied to the place, so any buried acorn within `DIG_RESPONSE_IN_SIZES` of the beak comes up) and then eats or buries what it got. A dug-up spot is remembered too, since birds often bury near each other, even though it may now be empty. A site stays remembered after a successful dig and is dropped only when a dig by this NPC within range of it comes up empty.
- **Flying to a ground target** (gather, retrieve, find burial spot, idle trips) follows a diagonal path and touches down on the target itself. From the ground the bird climbs diagonally (the slope up and down is `GLIDE_SLOPE`, 0.45) and starts the glide down as soon as it reaches the descent diagonal onto the target (it does not have to reach any cruising height first); if the trip is long enough it levels off at about one bird-size and comes down the diagonal later. A bird that starts high keeps its height until the diagonal reaches it. A bird that is above the diagonal and too close for that (above the target, or near it) flies straight at the target in a straight line, across and down: straight down if it is right above it. It never flies away from the target. The straight line is flown smoothly: the bird aims at the target every frame and moves sideways and down together, with the faster axis throttled (`Npc.scaleX` / `scaleY`, passed to `updatePlayer`) so the path is a true line instead of alternating between moving across and dropping. A bird already down at the ground does not wait for the line (a target whose ground drops away faster than the line would leave it waiting forever): it just moves across. Heights are measured above the target's ground level, so hillsides do not fool the glide. Targets nearer than 4.5 bird sizes (`FLY_MIN_DISTANCE_IN_SIZES`) are reached by hopping, not flying: a takeoff always rises about a bird size first.
- Each NPC has random `nosiness` and `secrecy` (0-1, shown above it with its hunger). **Nosiness** makes it follow other birds (see `follow`). **Secrecy** sets how many times it hops over a fresh burial site (see `cover`): secrecy x 10 hops rounded up (at least 1, at most 10); it also decides where it buries: a spot must have a burial quality above the bird's secrecy.
- **Burial quality** (`burialQuality` in `burial.ts`, 0-1, for a bird looking to bury at a spot): starts at 1; each other bird (the player too) within half a screen width of the spot, wherever the burying bird is, lowers it by 0.8 if that bird can see the spot (the same view used for finding acorns and noticing digs: about 4.7 sizes ahead, 1 behind) and by 0.4 if it is within half a screen of the spot but cannot see it; an oak lowers it by up to 0.5 (at its trunk centre, linearly to 0 at the farthest an acorn can fall: half the width of its leaf clusters, `ACORN_SPREAD_FACTOR`); a place where the burier itself remembers burying an acorn (`Player.burials`) lowers it by 0.2 at the place, easing linearly to 0 two dig tolerances (2/3 of a bird size) away (`BURIAL_QUALITY_OWN_SITE_PENALTY`; the player's heatmap uses the player's own list); within dig range of a remembered burial site of another bird lowers it by 0.9 (`BURIAL_QUALITY_OTHER_SITE_PENALTY`; the owner may come to dig there and would get the new acorn; for the player's heatmap those are the spots where the player dug up an acorn that was not its own, until a later dig there comes up empty: `Player.dugSites`); a spot with no way to bury it scores 0 (`buryingFacings`: the beak tip must not be over the creek's water, and the bird must have a dry place to stand one beak reach behind it, facing either way; a bird buries only with its beak tip on dry ground, the player too); never below 0. When facing the spot the way it arrives would put the bird over the water (a spot at the water's edge approached from the creek side), the NPC walks round to the other side of the spot and faces the creek instead (`moveBeakTo`, which does this for digging up sites too). There is no smoothing. A bird can judge spots up to half a screen width either side of itself. The quality of the spot it buries at is shown above its head for 4 seconds from when it starts burying.
- **Hiding burial mounds:** any bird (the player too) that starts a hop within 1.1 bird sizes of a buried acorn kicks 3 leaves onto its mound and counts a hop for it; the mound flattens and fades smoothly toward the ground's colour (`Acorn.conceal` eases toward (hops / 10) ^ 2.2, so early hops change the mound little and the last ones the most) and after the tenth hop is no longer drawn at all. Every hop on the ground kicks up leaves and disturbs (shrinks, sinks and fades to the ground colour) older kicked leaves nearby, replacing them with new ones, and the ground starts with kicked-up leaves in piles everywhere, so a pile of kicked leaves never marks a burial. The acorn is still buried there and can still be dug up.
- **Follow** (jays only; `considerFollow`, `decideFollow`, `runFollow`): an idle NPC, or one exploring, that remembers no free acorn (it may remember other birds' burial sites), is not starving (hunger at least `NPC_RETRIEVE_HUNGER`, 1/3) and is not holding or doing anything, rolls for the birds in its perception region (the player and the other jays): the moment it becomes eligible it rolls once for each bird already in view, one at a time, those carrying an acorn first; the first roll that succeeds starts the following and ends the sequence, and if every roll fails it does not follow (the birds in view count as seen, so it rolls again only for a bird entering its view later, or after becoming ineligible and eligible again); after that it rolls for each bird that *enters* its view: `0.5 x nosiness`, or `nosiness` if that bird is carrying an acorn. A bird in the follow state cannot be followed, and a bird that is being followed does not start following. Following: it moves to within a random distance of two to four bird widths (`FOLLOW_DISTANCE_MIN_IN_SIZES` / `FOLLOW_DISTANCE_MAX_IN_SIZES`, uniform, drawn when it starts following) of the bird and faces it. If the target flies it takes off and flies along, changing altitude now and then while moving sideways like explore and hovering otherwise; if the target is standing it lands beside it (flying to it first when it is far, otherwise hopping). If it follows a bird without an acorn and a bird carrying one is in view it switches to that bird. Leaving: each second, the target more than half a screen (`BURIAL_QUALITY_RANGE_IN_SIZES`) away, then back to idle or explore by hunger (the Fed? diamond); a free acorn in view, or noticing a burial site (a new one, or one it already knew that comes back into view; a known site already in view when it began following does not count; seeing another bird dig an acorn up never counts, even though it notes the spot as a site, `BurialSite.fromDig`, while seeing one bury an acorn does): gather; hunger below `NPC_RETRIEVE_HUNGER` (1/3): retrieve if it remembers a burial of its own, otherwise explore (it always stops following). And the patience rule: when the target has stayed inside a horizontal window one bird width wide (`PATIENCE_WINDOW_IN_SIZES`) for `PATIENCE_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (5 s) the follower rolls with probability 1 - nosiness to lose interest (then idle or explore by hunger), and again every interval while it stays in the window; the window restarts when the target leaves it. After leaving a bird it does not follow that bird again for `FOLLOW_COOLDOWN_SECONDS` (10 s). With the debug display on (the NPC labels above the birds, the minimap and the follow arrows are all shown only then), a pink arrow runs from each follower to its target. There is no hide state.

### Acorn supply

Each second every oak has a small chance (`ACORN_DROP_CHANCE_PER_SECOND`, times (birds + squirrels) / birds because the squirrels need acorns too) to drop a group of 2-5 acorns from random points in its canopy, in the middle half of its width (`ACORN_SPREAD_FACTOR` = 0.5); they fall under gravity and become ordinary free acorns.

### Impossible goals

NPCs (jays and squirrels) never choose a goal they cannot carry out, and do not stay stuck on one that fails anyway:

- **Feasibility filter** (`canGoTo` in `npc.ts`): a place needing the beak (a free acorn to pick up, another bird's burial site to dig, a remembered burial to retrieve, a spot to bury at) is a possible goal only if it is dry land, the bird has a dry place to stand to reach it (`standingFacings`, `burial.ts`), and it is not a place the NPC recently gave up on. The filter applies to noticed acorns, noticed sites, retrieval (the very-hungry trip only starts if a feasible burial exists), the explore heading toward remembered places, and burial spot selection. Idle trips never end in the creek.
- **Standing side** (`moveBeakTo`): whatever the beak target, the NPC stands where its body is dry, facing the way that works (toward the target as it arrives, or the other way if that would put it over water).
- **Watchdog** (`watchGoal`): the same goal (state and target place) pursued for `GOAL_GIVE_UP_SECONDS` (40) is dropped, the place is ignored for `GOAL_BLOCKED_SECONDS` (90) (`Npc.blocked`), and the NPC goes back to exploring or idling (or, when looking for a burial spot, looks for another one). A change of goal restarts the clock.

## NPC squirrel behaviour reference (moved out of SQUIRREL_STATE_MACHINE.md)

### How a squirrel differs from a blue jay

- **One per oak.** Every oak tree has one squirrel (`createSquirrels`, `world.squirrels`). It is the same size as the jay (`SQUIRREL_SIZE_FRACTION` = 1): its body is as long as a jay's (nose to rump), gray with a pale belly, and its slim bushy tail has an S-shaped sweep and adds 0.8 of a body length when stretched out fully; in motion it is raised over the back (curled up behind it when the squirrel sits upright to eat, trailing when it climbs). Its haunch and big hind foot look the same in every pose; only the body (which tips up to sit upright) and the tail change.
- **Never flies.** All movement is hopping on the ground (`npc.keys.w` is forced off), at `SQUIRREL_SPEED_SCALE` (1.0 of the player's walking speed; jays use 0.75). Anything a jay would fly to (a far acorn, a burial spot) a squirrel hops to.
- **A fuzzy home stretch.** `home = { tree, trunkX, left, right }`: the ground within the reach of its oak (`acornReach`: the oak's acorns land within half the width of its leaf clusters, `ACORN_SPREAD_FACTOR` = 0.5) on either side of the trunk. It **explores** only inside it (turning round at the edges), and it **buries** only inside it (candidate burial points outside the stretch are never chosen; `inHome`). But it perceives everything in view, also outside the stretch, and **ventures out** to gather any free acorn or other animal's burial site it sees there (gather, retrieve of its own burials, idle's trip to the trunk are not limited). Whenever it is outside the stretch (by more than 0.3 of its size) and is in explore or looking for a burial spot, it walks back toward it (`returnHome`) instead: no exploring, no sniffing and no burial search until it is back inside. On the way back it still notices acorns in view and, at the once-per-second check, can go gather another one (and eats it if hungry); if it picked an acorn up and is not hungry, it walks back before looking for a spot to bury it.
- **Noticing is acted on at once.** When it notices a free acorn or a burial site (also by sniffing; a sniff in progress finishes first), the decision runs immediately, so it gathers it instead of wandering out of view first. While exploring with nothing in view it heads toward the nearest acorn or burial site it remembers inside its home stretch (turning round if it must), until the spot is in view again. (Jays now do the same.)
- **No nosiness.** Squirrels have no nosiness score (`Npc.nosiness` is null, no 'nosy' bar in their label) and no **follow** state is planned for them. There is no hide state (neither for squirrels nor for jays).
- **Idle = up the tree.** A squirrel starts idling when its hunger is above 0.8 (`SQUIRREL_IDLE_ENTER_HUNGER`) and gets up again when it falls below 0.7 (`SQUIRREL_IDLE_LEAVE_HUNGER`), so it does not flip between the two (a jay uses 0.9 for both). An acorn fills only a sixth of a squirrel's bar, so it rarely gets above 0.83: with 0.9 / 0.8 a squirrel idled about 1% of the time in simulation, with 0.8 / 0.7 about 20%. In **idle** it walks to the foot of its home trunk and climbs up the side of the trunk (animation, `SQUIRREL_CLIMB_SPEED_IN_SIZES` = 4.8 sizes per second) **all the way up out of the play area** (its body entirely above the top edge of the world); it is then **hidden** (`bird.hidden`): not drawn, perceives nothing, not perceived by anyone, not counted for burial quality. The oak's leaves are drawn in front of a squirrel climbing its trunk (`drawOakCrown`, a crown-only bitmap clipped around the squirrel). It stays up there until it leaves idle, then climbs down the trunk (animation) and starts exploring. Squirrels start with a random hunger from 0.7 to 0.9 (jays 0.4 to 1); those that start above 0.8 begin hidden in their tree.
- **Hunger.** The bar is twice as large (`SQUIRREL_HUNGER_SCALE` = 2): it drains in 120 s instead of 60 s and an acorn fills 1/6 of it instead of 1/3 (`Player.hungerScale`). The thresholds are still fractions of the bar (eat below 2/3, retrieve below 1/3, idle above 0.8 and leave idle below 0.7). Each squirrel needs the same number of acorns per second as a jay, so the acorn drop chance and the world cap are multiplied by (birds + squirrels) / birds in `updateTreeDrops`.
- **Secrecy is always 0.5** (`SQUIRREL_SECRECY`): it hops over a fresh burial 5 times, and only buries at spots with burial quality above 0.5. **Oaks are ignored in its burial quality** (`burialQuality(..., useOaks = false)`).
- **Hop pause.** A squirrel stands still for a random 0.1 to 0.4 s (`SQUIRREL_HOP_PAUSE_MIN_SECONDS` / `SQUIRREL_HOP_PAUSE_MAX_SECONDS`) after every hop before it hops again (jays hop continuously), but only while it **explores with no destination or searches for a burial spot**. On the way to somewhere it hops without pausing: to a chosen burial spot, back to its home stretch, to an acorn or burial site it has seen or remembers (gather, retrieve, exploring toward a remembered acorn or site), or to its trunk. Its legs only move while it is actually hopping (`squirrelHopRest` in npc.ts). This rest is part of the hop physics (`hopOnGround`, `Player.hopRest`), so it also holds while a movement key stays pressed through the landing.
- **Sniffing.** While exploring, a squirrel hops 2, 3 or 4 times (`SQUIRREL_SNIFF_EVERY_HOPS_MIN/MAX`; the hop that makes the count still travels as normal; the key is let go just before it lands so no further hop starts), pauses and sniffs for `SQUIRREL_SNIFF_SECONDS` (0.9 s), nose to the ground and the head hardly moving, then **keeps exploring the same way**. (Burying and digging up are different: the head works up and down and the two front paws scratch the ground; picking an acorn up is one smooth stoop. All head movements ease in and out so the head never jumps.) When a sniff starts, every buried acorn of another animal within one squirrel width of its nose (`SQUIRREL_SNIFF_RADIUS_IN_SIZES` = 1, measured from the nose tip) gets one roll to be noticed, with the usual chance (it falls with the hops that disguised the mound) **doubled** (`SQUIRREL_SNIFF_NOTICE_FACTOR` = 2, capped at 1): 100% for a fresh mound, 44% at 3 hops, and never below 20% (`SQUIRREL_SNIFF_MIN_CHANCE`), even for a completely hidden mound. Simulated with fully hidden mounds spread over the stretch and a squirrel that does nothing but explore, it finds a given mound after a median of 31 s (90% within 110 s, 98% within 5 minutes); real squirrels also eat, bury and rest, so it takes longer. A noticed mound joins the squirrel's list of other animals' burial sites like any noticed site, and the dashed notice line is drawn from its eye.
- **Its nose is the beak.** Burying and digging act at the nose tip: 44 sprite units ahead of its centre (`SQUIRREL_NOSE_REACH_X`, scaled by size). The dig range (1/4 of a bird size) and everything derived from it scale with the squirrel's own size.
- **Interacting with jays.** Squirrels and jays (and the player) are all "birds" as far as perception goes: a visible squirrel counts for a jay's burial quality, jays and squirrels notice each other's burials and digs, they compete for free acorns, and everyone's hops kick leaves onto mounds. Only the squirrel's hidden/climbing state changes that: a hidden squirrel is ignored.

## Mid-winter frame cost
- Measured in the (throttled) pane: the JavaScript side of a frame is about the same in mid winter as in summer (about 3 ms against 2.3 in the same world); the extra cost is in what the canvas has to paint, which the pane cannot show. So the winter passes were cut:
  - Under a complete blanket of snow (`thickness` and `cover` both 1) the dirt ground and the leaf litter are not drawn at all (`renderScene`, `blanket`); the snow polygon covers the same area.
  - The far layers' ground (bitmap + half-size snow mask + full-size haze mask: three passes over each layer's visible ground) is drawn in one pass once the snow and the haze are complete and nothing is melting (`snowedIn`, `ForestStrip.frozenCanvas` / `frozenColor`: the ground's shape at 0.75 size in its final color, made lazily, at most 2 a frame), and only the fill below it besides; the masks are used again for the transitions and the melt.
  - A foreground rock's snow heap path is built once and kept (`RockSnowOverlay.heap`) instead of a 200-point line every frame.

- **Blue outline on far ridges in early winter:** `makeMask` drew the ground's shape five times (once and a pixel left, right, up, down) to widen it, which also multiplied the opacity of the soft edge pixels, so the haze (blue) and snow (white) masks showed as an outline along each ridge against the sky. The masks (haze, snow, frozen) are now made from a single draw (`grow` 0).

- Boulder dusting is fully opaque along the rock's top (gradient: opaque to 30%, 0.45 at 70%, 0 at the end, starting at 1.08 ry above the base) so the heap line that starts to grow over it does not stand out as a brighter line.

- **Brown line along the snow surface while the snow is translucent:** the dirt polygon's top edge (brownish) showed through the thin snow as a line; while snow covers all the ground the dirt's top edge is 4 px lower than before (`drawGround` `sink`) and the snow's is 2 px above the surface.
- **Snowfall stays with the world:** flakes now fall through the world and are placed against the camera in y as well as x (shifted by `camera.y * depth`, wrapping the screen), so flying up or down changes how fast the snow seems to fall just as it does for the rest of the scene (`drawSnowfall(..., cameraX, cameraY, ...)`).

- **Ground snow as a color change (replaces the 4 px / 2 px offsets):** while snow covers all of the foreground ground the dirt polygon's gradient is mixed toward the snow's (`drawGround`, `whiteness` = thickness while cover is 1) and the litter fades by `drawLeafLitter`'s alpha (1 - thickness), so there is no second layer with an edge of its own; `drawSnow` now only draws the melting patches (cover < 1) over the dirt. The dirt is drawn again in mid winter (it is the snow), the snow polygon's top is back at the surface - 1.

- **Far-layer snow at the start of the melt:** the patches' rows started at the layer's base line, so the ridges above it lost their snow the instant the melt began (the flat "snowed in" ground was replaced by patches that did not reach them). `ForestStrip.rise` (how far the ground rises above the base line at the highest, found when the strip is built) now sets the first row of patches, so they cover the whole ground at the start of the melt and only then break up.

- **Brown fringe on far ridges while the snow fades in:** the far layers' ground bitmaps (a separate bitmap per layer, with their own soft, brownish edge pixels) are tinted by masks of their shape. A mask drawn once leaves an edge pixel with half its coverage only half tinted, so the brownish edge shows as a thin line along the ridge until the layer's flat "snowed in" ground takes over at the start of mid winter. `makeMask` now draws the shape on itself three times (`repeats`): soft edge pixels become more opaque (0.5 -> 0.875), nothing spreads outward, so there is neither the brown fringe nor the blue/white outline the widened masks made. Only the far layers could have this line; the foreground ground has no second layer any more.

- **Far-layer ground tinted by color, not by masks:** while the snow and haze come in or the snow melts, a far layer's ground bitmap is drawn onto a screen-sized scratch canvas (`tintCanvas`), tinted there with `source-atop` fills (snow, inside the melt's patches when melting, then the haze), and the result drawn as one piece. The tint changes the color of every painted pixel including the soft edge pixels, in the same share as the rest, so the ridge has no separate brownish edge showing (the masks drawn over the bitmap left one). The haze and snow mask canvases are gone (less memory); only the flat `frozenCanvas` for the complete-snow state is kept. Same idea as the foreground ground: its own color changes and the litter fades.
- **Boulder snow melts continuously:** the overlay's melt size is now in steps of 1/160 (was 1/24: visible jumps) and the dusting/cap fades in 1/48, and the cap shrinks from the sides into a patch over the rock's top as well as getting shallower (`snowGeometry`: `reach` = rx x (0.15 + 1.4 x size), thinner toward its ends, `squash`), like the ground's patches. The far layers' rocks still change from moment image to moment image (keypoints).

- **Boulder snow drawn as vector shapes every frame:** the foreground rocks no longer keep a snow overlay bitmap rebuilt in steps. `silhouetteColumns` finds once per rock, column by column, where its bitmap's painted pixels start and end (360 columns, a little smoothed); `drawRockSnow` fills the dusting and the full cap as shapes between the silhouette's top (1.5 px above) and their lower edges (not below the silhouette's bottom), and strokes the heap along the top, every frame, so the melt's shrinking is continuous. The snow shrinks to nothing: its depth scales to 0 below a size of 0.3 and its reach from the middle to 0.02 rx (`snowGeometry`), and the drawing only stops at a size of 0.003. The far layers' rocks keep the per-moment images (`paintRockWithSnow`).
- **No jump of the snow level at the start of the melt:** the patches stand 3 px above the surface, the snow-colored ground did not; the ground's top now rises 3 px (smoothstep) over the 30 s before the melt starts (`drawGround` `raise`).

- **Boulder melt earlier, in patches from the bottom up:** each rock's snow melts over 0.6 of a segment starting between 5.85 and 6.15 (`snowCapSize`: before the ground's melt at 6.0 and all gone by 6.75, long before the ground's patches at 7.4). The cap no longer draws in from the sides (which left a vertical slice at the end): along the rock each place has its own point of the melt (`snowGeometry` `pattern`, a smooth pattern across the rock) and the cap's thickness there (`thick(x)`, a smoothstep of the size against that point) goes to nothing, so the lower edge rises in patches, the last bits shrinking away. The heap is now a ribbon along the smoothed contour (11-point average, not a stroked polyline) whose width follows `thick(x)` and fades toward the rock's sides, so it thins away smoothly with the cap instead of being cut into stepped pieces.

- Boulder snow melts slower and later: from between 5.9 and 6.1 to between 6.7 and 7.05 (the ground's cover is then 0.5 to 0.25), and the patch pattern is gentler (`pattern` has two slow waves, `thick` a wider range 0.4/0.6) so the edges are less steep.
